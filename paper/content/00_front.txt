@title Resilient Data Science Programming for Fault-Tolerant Distributed Analytics in Multi-Cluster Spark Environments

@abstract
Apache Spark survives the loss of a task or an executor, but a job is still lost when its whole cluster fails. We present RMC-Spark, a resilience layer over several independent Spark clusters that moves a failed data science pipeline to a healthy cluster and is designed to resume it from its last committed stage. A health monitor scores each cluster from five signals. An adaptive policy checkpoints a stage once unsaved work reaches the Young and Daly interval, using a running estimate of the mean time between failures. A controller moves a job only when it has stopped progressing, a cost function chooses the target, and every decision is logged with its cause. A small Python library turns PySpark functions into idempotent stages committed to shared object storage. We built the system with Docker, Spark 3.5, MinIO and Prometheus, and 56 automated tests check its logic. In 36 runs on a two-cluster testbed, RMC-Spark detected each full cluster outage in about 8 s, moved the job and finished both valid outage runs, while single-cluster configurations failed all five of theirs. Fault-free runs showed no measurable overhead, against 24 % for fixed checkpointing, because the jobs were too short for the adaptive policy to checkpoint.

@unnumbered Executive Summary

Fraud checks and production-line alarms cannot wait for a rerun [@zhou2021internet, @rosati2022from], and Spark is the usual engine for such work [@zaharia2016apache, @tang2020survey]. Its fault tolerance, however, ends at the cluster boundary: a dead master or a lost zone stops every job on that cluster [@zaharia2012rdd, @isukapalli2024systematic]. Our rds library makes pipeline stages resumable, and our controller moves stalled jobs to a healthy cluster and explains why. Table {tab:summary} lists the contributions and their status.

The gain is that an outage which now ends in a failed job and a manual rerun becomes an automatic, explained recovery. In our runs RMC-Spark caught every outage within about 8 s and finished the job on the healthy cluster, while single-cluster setups lost every such job at the deadline; fault-free runs were no slower than plain Spark. Our jobs were short, so the adaptive policy never wrote a checkpoint; resuming long pipelines still has to be measured (Section XXI).
