@title Resilient Data Science Programming for Fault-Tolerant Distributed Analytics in Multi-Cluster Spark Environments

@abstract
Distributed analytics platforms such as Apache Spark recover well from the loss of individual tasks, but an analytics job still fails when the cluster that runs it becomes unavailable, and long lineage chains make recovery slow even when the cluster survives. This paper presents RMC-Spark, a resilience layer that coordinates several independent Spark clusters and makes data science pipelines resumable across them. RMC-Spark combines five mechanisms: a health monitor that scores each cluster from five signals with an exponentially weighted anomaly detector; an adaptive checkpointing policy that saves a pipeline stage once the unsaved work reaches the Young and Daly optimum interval derived from an online estimate of the mean time between failures; a progress-aware failover controller that migrates only stalled jobs; a cost-based placement function; and an explainable reliability score that records the cause of every recovery decision. A small programming library turns ordinary PySpark functions into idempotent stages whose committed outputs live in shared object storage, so a job resubmitted on another cluster restores finished stages instead of recomputing them. The system is implemented with Docker, Spark 3.5, MinIO, Prometheus and a Python controller, and its decision logic is verified by 56 automated tests, including a simulated cluster outage. An evaluation methodology with nine reproducible fault types, four workloads and five compared configurations is specified together with statistical tests. Measured results are reported in Section XV once the experiment matrix has been executed. The design targets banking, healthcare, manufacturing and other domains in which analytics downtime has direct business cost.

@unnumbered Executive Summary

Organisations increasingly depend on distributed analytics for decisions that cannot wait, from fraud screening to production-line monitoring [@zhou2021internet, @rosati2022from]. Apache Spark has become the common engine for such workloads because one programming model covers batch, streaming, SQL and machine learning [@zaharia2016apache, @tang2020survey]. Its built-in fault tolerance, however, is scoped to a single cluster: lost partitions are recomputed from lineage and failed tasks are retried, but a failed master, a network partition or a lost availability zone stops every job that depends on that cluster [@zaharia2012rdd, @isukapalli2024systematic].

This work treats resilience as a property of the data science program and of the platform together. On the programming side, the proposed rds library lets an analyst mark pipeline stages; each stage becomes idempotent, records its timing, and is checkpointed to shared storage according to an adaptive policy. On the platform side, a controller observes all clusters, explains its assessment of each one, moves jobs away from a failed cluster only when they have actually stopped making progress, and resumes them from their last committed stage elsewhere. The expected industrial value lies in converting outages that currently end in failed jobs and manual reruns into automatic, explained recoveries with bounded lost work. Whether that expectation holds, and at what overhead, is decided by the experiments defined in Sections XIII and XIV; the numbers are deliberately not anticipated in this summary.
